What are BMR and TDEE?

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body uses at complete rest to keep your heart beating, breathing and temperature steady. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is BMR plus everything you burn moving and exercising: the calories you actually use in a day. Eat about your TDEE and your weight stays roughly the same; eat less and you lose weight; eat more and you gain.

Which formula is used?

By default the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most widely used and generally most accurate estimate. If you enter your body fat percentage, the calculator switches to Katch-McArdle, which works from lean body mass and suits people with more muscle.

How big should a fat-loss deficit be?

Start 300–500 kcal below TDEE; this page uses 400 kcal, roughly 0.4 kg (0.9 lb) a week. Bigger deficits cost muscle and are hard to sustain. Eating enough protein (about 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight) and keeping up strength training helps you keep muscle.

How much extra to build muscle?

About 200–300 kcal above TDEE, with progressive strength training. A bigger surplus mostly adds fat. Gaining about 0.5–1 kg (1–2 lb) a month is a reasonable pace.

How accurate is this?

Every formula is an estimate, and people differ by 10–20%. Follow the target for two to three weeks, track your weight, then adjust by 100–200 kcal at a time. This page is for information only and is not medical or dietary advice.
